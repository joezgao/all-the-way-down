How it works

Each surfel is a flat Gaussian disk that also stores its native scale, s = depth / focal of the camera that made it (Eq. 1). The five levels were made by cameras ten times closer each time, all aimed at one stoma.

While you zoom, each surfel's opacity is multiplied by α (Eq. 2–3), which interpolates in log scale: 1 at its own scale, falling to 0 at its parent's or child's. Between two levels their α add to 1 (Prop. 1), so detail cross-fades instead of popping.

Surfels with α below 1/255 are dropped before sorting, so the GPU only holds what this zoom needs. Turn modulation off to draw every level at once, not the paper's retrained ablation. Memory is 32 B per drawn surfel plus 12 B per (surfel, 16 px tile) pair, the lists a tile rasterizer like 3DGS would allocate.

The scene is procedural, not WonderZoom's: their released scenes are 0.3–1.8 GB PyTorch files that need a CUDA renderer. The leaf here is maths, prefiltered at each level's pixel size. The renderer, the leaf and the feather that keeps Prop. 1 true at the edge of each local patch of detail are not from the paper.
